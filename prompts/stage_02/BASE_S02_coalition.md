# Stage 2 — Coalition Synthesis (base)

Stage 2 is **not** clustering and **not** distillation. It is **coalition
synthesis**: a small group of expert lenses bring one raw idea each into
a structured discussion, and try to derive a deeper theory than any of
the input ideas alone.

## What "deeper" actually means

A deep theory is one where **the participating ideas could not have
produced the deeper claim by simple addition or umbrella framing**. A
deep theory should:

- name a mechanism, principle, or architecture that **explains why the
  input ideas were each saying something true** about a shared
  underlying structure;
- be **specific enough to make a non-trivial prediction** that none of
  the input ideas would have predicted on its own;
- **preserve, not erase, important tensions** between the input ideas;
- be **traceable** back to the contributing ideas.

A deep theory is **not**:

- a compatibility umbrella ("all of these are special cases of learning");
- a list of bullet points stitched together with "and";
- a vague meta-claim ("the brain does X via predictive Y");
- a re-statement of one input idea with the others mentioned in passing.

## Roleplay discipline

When the facilitator simulates a multi-round expert discussion, each
expert must speak in their own voice and from their own tools. Do not
let one expert's vocabulary erase the others. The discussion should
contain genuine clarification, genuine objection, and genuine
synthesis — not a polite agreement that converges to whichever idea
sounded most prestigious.

## Preservation marks

Some input ideas carry preservation marks (`UNIQUE`, `RISKY`,
`SPECIAL`). These marks signal that the idea is structurally
distinctive and should remain visible in any synthesis that involves
it. Do not bury marked material in a generic synthesis.

## Framework aids

The shared **Framework aids** prompt (above) offers classical lenses
from the philosophy of science — Marr's three levels, Tinbergen's
four questions, descriptive/mechanistic/normative,
cybernetic/control-theoretic, dynamical systems, plus Popper
falsifiability, Occam parsimony, and explanatory-vs-predictive
power. Use them when they sharpen your synthesis; ignore them when
they do not. Do NOT force a synthesis into a framework checklist —
the "aids, not bins" discipline overrides everything else.
